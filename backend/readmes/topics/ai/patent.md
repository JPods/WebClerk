FIELD OF THE INVENTION 
The invention relates to intermodal freight logistics, and more particularly to systems and methods for transporting cargo using railroads, automated guideway transit (AGT) networks, and local-use vehicles (LUVs, such as pickups, vans, and cargobikes) integrated into a three-tier logistics architecture enabling energy-efficient, sensor-tracked, and AI-coordinated transport.

BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION
Conventional freight logistics rely heavily on long-haul trucking, contributing to congestion, infrastructure wear, and high energy use. While railroads and automated systems exist, they are poorly integrated, resulting in inefficient transfers and limited last-mile accessibility. There remains a need for a unified logistics framework that combines long-haul, middle-mile, and last-mile systems with standardized containers, predictive coordination, and digital chain-of-custody assurance.

SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION
This invention integrates railroads, automated guideway transit (AGT) networks, and local-use vehicles (LUVs, pickups, cargobikes, etc…) into a unified three-tier logistics system. Standardized subcontainers are compatible across all modes, equipped with sensor suites for environmental and positional monitoring. Blockchain-based records ensure verifiable chain of custody, and large language models (LLMs) optimize scheduling of loading, unloading, and routing. The invention reduces transport energy consumption, minimizes pavement damage, and enhances logistics resilience.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION
The system forms a physical and digital logistics web across three tiers. The long-haul tier includes trains, aircraft, or tubes (FIG 1, 3) that move standardized subcontainers between cities. The middle-mile tier comprises AGT networks (FIG 1, 2) linking rail terminals to neighborhoods through grade-separated guideways and autonomous vehicles. The last-mile tier uses LUVs (FIG 1, 1) for delivery within neighborhoods. Subcontainers equipped with sensors and smart-locking systems ensure cargo integrity and traceability. Blockchain integration records custody events, and LLMs analyze operational data to optimize throughput and schedule synchronization.

CLAIMS
1. A system of transport integrating railroads, automated guideway transit (AGT) networks, and local-use vehicles (LUVs) into a three-tier logistics network, comprising:
– a long-haul tier configured to move standardized subcontainers between cities by railroads, aircraft, or tube networks;
– a middle-mile tier comprising grade-separated AGT guideways and autonomous vehicles configured to transport the subcontainers within cities; and
– a last-mile tier comprising LUVs configured to deliver subcontainers to local destinations;
wherein the system reduces reliance on highway trucking and associated energy consumption by coordinating transfers of the subcontainers across tiers.
2. A system of claim 1, wherein standardized subcontainers compatible across multiple transportation modes, comprising:
– optimized dimensions and payload capacities for AGT and LUV transport;
– features allowing aggregation into pallets and standard railroad, aircraft, and tube-network containers;
– modular design enabling variable shapes and capacities; and
– smart-tracking and monitoring components configured to record location, environmental, and custody data.
3. A system of claim 1, wherein blockchain-based logistics platform comprising:
– real-time tracking of inventory and location data;
– verifiable chain-of-custody records;
– permissioned access to shipping information;
– integration of image or sensor logs documenting cargo condition at each custody transfer; and
– smart-contract modules configured to automate shipping agreements.
4. A system of claim 1, wherein one or more large-language models (LLMs) are configured to define load and unload windows for long-haul container movements, the windows specifying at least load and unload times, cargo classifications, hazard requirements, volume (cube) limits, and gross-weight restrictions, and to generate corresponding natural-language shipment data enabling non-expert users to identify and utilize available shipping windows.
5. A system of claim 1, wherein each subcontainer includes at least one inflatable air bladder configured to stabilize the contents during transit and to detect unauthorized openings.
6. A system of claim 1, wherein each subcontainer includes a sensor suite comprising at least one sensor selected from the group consisting of an accelerometer, a temperature sensor, a geolocation sensor, and a tamper-detection sensor.
7. A system of claim 1, wherein the blockchain-based system is configured to log data from the sensor suite of claim 6 to create an immutable record of events including impacts, temperature excursions, location changes, and tamper attempts, thereby providing enhanced chain-of-custody verification for commercial logistics operations.
8. A system of claim 1, further comprising one or more routing-control modules configured to schedule the movement of subcontainers on the AGT network in accordance with LLM-defined load and unload windows for coordinated delivery timing.
9. A system of claim 1, wherein the large-language model (LLM) is configured to accumulate and organize operational data from multiple sources to generate predictive shipment forecasts that combine private user data with system-level logistics data for optimization of shipments for customers and carriers.
10. A system of claim 1, wherein the data-storage system is configured to encrypt private data such that only authorized users possessing encryption keys can access the encrypted information.
11. A system of claim 1, further comprising racking and material-handling structures configured to load subcontainers into local-use vehicles (LUVs) and to aggregate the subcontainers onto pallets or ISO containers for transfer to higher-tier transport systems.
12. A system of claim 1, further comprising one or more reporting modules configured to generate environmental-benefit and carbon-credit reports based on measured shipment energy-use metrics.

ABSTRACT 
A system and method integrate railroads, automated guideway transit (AGT) networks, and local-use vehicles (LUVs) into a three-tier logistics chain. Standardized subcontainers, compatible across all tiers, may include adaptive air bladders and sensor suites (accelerometer, temperature, geolocation, tamper detection) for cargo stabilization and monitoring. A blockchain ledger records sensor data for verifiable chain of custody, while machine learning optimizes routing and aggregation. Large language models (LLMs) define load and unload windows for inter-city containers, enabling AGT networks to stream subcontainers for just-in-time compliance. The system reduces energy use to below 45 percent of highway trucking, decreases pavement damage, and increases logistics resilience. Unlike prior systems, the invention unites grade-separated AGT middle-mile transport with secure, sensor-monitored subcontainers and LLM-scheduled operations.
