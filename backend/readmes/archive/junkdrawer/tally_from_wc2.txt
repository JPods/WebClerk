What WC2 Does — The Tally Architecture

  Three distinct layers of data→information:

  1. Line Extension (data → extended data)

  OrdLnExtend, IvcLnExtend, PoLnExtend, PpLnExtend — these take a single
  line item and compute:
  - Backlog qty (ordered - shipped)
  - Discounted unit price (via DiscountApply)
  - Extended price, extended cost, extended weight
  - Tax per line (via TaxCalcLine)
  - Commission per line (via CM_LineCalc — supports both
  sales-amount-based and margin-based)
  - Handles unit-of-measure conversions (*10 meaning price-per-10)

  2. Header Rollup (extended data → document totals)

  calcOrder, calcInvoice, calcPO, calcProposal — loop all lines, sum
  extensions into header fields:
  - amount, totalCost, grossMargin, salesTax, taxOnCost, shipTotal, total
  - Commission (with per-rep scriptable override via ExecuteText)
  - Auto-freight calculation
  - Multi-currency with exchange rate
  - Running balance against customer credit limit
  - Balance due = total - appliedDiscount - appliedAmount (invoice)

  3. Cross-Document Consolidation (header totals → customer/GL/inventory
  intelligence)

  - Ledger_TallyBal — AR aging buckets (future, current, 30/60/90) from
  unAppliedValue on ledger records, plus avg days paid, invoice count,
  total exposure (open orders + balance due), high credit watermark
  - GL_JrnlSummary — accumulates debits/credits by GL account + division,
  net-settles (if debit > credit, show net debit)
  - ConsolidateRecs — merges duplicate customer/item IDs across ALL
  related tables (orders, invoices, payments, contacts, serials, etc.)
  - ItemSiteTally — inventory by site/item, adjusts qty on hand from
  inventory transactions
  - DealLoc_Tally — aggregates invoice data by customer+type+ship-to into
  TallyResult records for dealer locator display

  4. TallyMaster — The Script Engine

  The TallyMaster table stores named, purpose-tagged scripts that run via
  ExecuteText. This is WC2's configurable calculation engine —
  users/admins could define custom tallies without code changes. Used for
  reports, email generation (EmailByTallyMaster), and custom business
  logic.

What's Actually Worth Salvaging from WC2                                
   
  1. Shipping/Freight — the real gap                                      
                                                                  
  WC2's ShippingCost is substantial: carrier profiles with zones,         
  weight-based rate tables (shipWtCost), fuel factors, per-package cost
  splitting via LoadTags, insurance calculation, COD charges, handling    
  charges, call tag rates. WC3 has nothing — only after-the-fact landed
  cost capture.

  Worth salvaging? The pattern, not the code. A Carrier model with zone   
  rates, fuel factor, and a scriptable override (same pattern as
  commission scripts in WC2).                                             
                                                                  
  2. Tax Jurisdiction — wire what exists

  WC2's TaxCalcLine shows three tax modes: StandardSalesTax (rate ×       
  amount), ByCost (rate × cost), and WebBased (external service call). WC3
   already has the TaxJurisdiction model with rate fields — it just needs 
  the calculation wired in. The WC2 pattern of jurisdiction-level 
  scriptable tax (aTaxScriptSales) is worth noting.

  Worth salvaging? The three-mode pattern. WC3 already has the model; it  
  needs the calc function.
                                                                          
  3. RunningBalance — credit check pattern                        

  WC2 checks credit limit minus balance due minus open orders in real time
   during document editing, with a visual alert (beep + yellow background)
   when over limit. WC3 has credit_utilization() but I don't know if it's 
  called during order/invoice entry.                              

  Worth salvaging? The trigger pattern — check credit at document save,   
  not just on a report.
                                                                          
  4. BOM cost rollup — already done                               

  WC3's recalc_parent_cost does what WC2's BOM_ExtendCost does. No salvage
   needed.
                                                                          
  5. Discount hierarchy                                                   
   
  WC2's Get_Price shows a clean precedence: item-specific discount →      
  type-of-sale price level (A/B/C/D/MSRP) → fallback. WC3's CatalogLine
  has tiers but the precedence logic may need confirming.                 
                                                                  
  Worth salvaging? The precedence rule, not the code.                     
   
  ---                                                                     
  Bottom line: Shipping/freight is the one real gap. Tax calc needs
  wiring. Credit check needs a trigger point. Everything else either      
  exists or is deferred (commission).

  1. Returns/RMA workflow — credit_note type exists but no return         
  authorization flow (create RMA → receive returned goods → issue credit
  note → adjust inventory). WC2 didn't have this formally either, but a   
  commerce app needs it.                                          
  2. Terms/early payment discount — "2% 10 net 30" calculation. terms
  field exists on transactions but no calc that applies the discount when 
  payment is within the discount window. WC2 had this via ShippingCost
  terms parameter.                                                        
  3. Customer statements — periodic AR statement generation (list of open
  invoices, payments, balance). Not the bank statement import (that       
  exists). The outbound statement you mail to the customer.
  4. Multi-currency on transactions — WC2 had exchangeRate and currency on
   every order/invoice. WC3 has currency in the cost envelope and         
  exchange_expense in finance, but no exchange rate field on the
  transaction header. No rate lookup.                                     
  5. Recurring orders/subscriptions — auto-generate orders on a schedule.
  Subscription commerce. Not present.                                     
  6. Drop ship — order line flagged to ship from vendor directly to
  customer. Automatically generates PO to vendor with customer's ship-to. 
  WC2 had DropShipFill.                                           
  7. Landed cost allocation — allocate freight, duty, customs costs across
   received items to compute true cost. InventoryLayer has the fields     
  (cost.freight, cost.duty) but no allocation service.
  8. Batch operations — batch invoice (invoice all open orders), batch    
  payment (apply one check to many invoices), batch ship. Individual      
  operations exist but no batch orchestration.
  9. Approval workflows — price below margin needs manager approval,      
  credit limit override needs approval, large orders need approval. No    
  workflow engine.
  10. Consignment — goods at customer location, billed when sold/used. No 
  model for this.
